PhD candidate in cosmology, Ariel University · available from January 2027

I study early-Universe signatures in gravitational waves and CMB.

I am a final-year PhD student at Ariel University, supervised by Prof. Ido Ben-Dayan. My research uses gravitational-wave and cosmological observations to test models of the early Universe, with particular emphasis on pulsar timing arrays, CMB and large-scale-structure data, and Bayesian model comparison. I am scheduled to defend my thesis in December 2026.

During my PhD, I have treated the pulsar timing array (PTA) signal as cosmological. In my most recent PTA work, I used the extra-radiation bounds placed by CMB experiments to rule out some of the cosmological explanations of the PTA signal (SIGWs, inflation, metastable cosmic strings, phase transitions), including forecasts of how Simons Observatory and CMB-S4 will constrain these models.

A major part of my PhD has focused on Bayesian model comparison against cosmological datasets. I built a Bayesian pipeline for the CMB lensing amplitude anomaly that scans across multipole ranges and dataset combinations, and found the anomaly is caused almost entirely by Planck's low multipoles, ℓ < 30. Removing them restores consistency with ΛCDM and greatly eases the S8 tension. I constrained the equation of state of non-cold dark matter using DESI DR2, isolating the preference for non-cold DM to BAO bins below z = 1.1; Planck and SN measurements favour positive and negative values respectively. In a paper submitted in August, I performed the full data analysis and model comparison for radiatively corrected Starobinsky inflation against ACT DR6 and DESI data.

I write my inference code in JAX, using differentiable particle filters and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo accelerated by learned surrogate Hamiltonians. I am currently studying how parameter estimates become biased when an isotropic template is used against an anisotropic background, and which template choices most compromise downstream constraints.

I have presented my work at international conferences and workshops, co-authored a LISA Consortium paper, and worked with research groups across the globe.
